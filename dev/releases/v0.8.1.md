# Airclone v0.8.1 — scrolling a folder full of photos no longer freezes the app

One bug, reported as: scrolling a folder with a lot of images and videos of
varying sizes locks up, and on Android the phone offers to close the app.

## What is fixed

- **Photo folders scroll properly again.** A folder of 128 mixed photos and
  videos went from stalling for up to half a second at a time — rendering
  somewhere between 11 and 33 frames a second — to a steady 54 to 61. Scrolling
  back over pictures you have already seen is now instant rather than being as
  expensive as the first pass.

- **The "Airclone isn't responding" dialog on Android is gone**, along with what
  caused it: roughly ten seconds of solid work on the one thread that also draws
  the screen, every time you opened a folder of pictures.

- **Folder previews** — the little four-up thumbnails on folders — were built on
  the same machinery and had the same two problems. They are fixed by the same
  change.

Thumbnails already saved on your device stay valid. Nothing is regenerated, and
nothing needs re-downloading.

## Notes for the curious

It was not the pictures. The cache Airclone keeps is encrypted on disk, and the
whole cost of that encryption was landing on the thread that draws the app.

Two separate faults, found by measuring on an Android emulator against 120
photos ranging from 640×480 to 6000×4000:

1. Unlocking the cache runs a deliberately slow key calculation — about a second
   and a half on a phone, which is the point of it. That result was remembered
   once it finished, but nothing stopped a *second* request starting its own
   while the first was still going. A folder of thumbnails asks all at once, so
   seven of them ran at the same time: **10.6 seconds** of the app being unable
   to respond to anything. That is the not-responding dialog, and it was a single
   missing piece of bookkeeping.

2. Every thumbnail read from the cache then decrypted on that same thread — a few
   milliseconds each, paid again for every tile that scrolled into view.

Both now happen on a separate worker, so neither can block the screen. And
thumbnails are kept in memory as well as on disk, which is why scrolling back is
now free: previously a picture scrolled off the screen was thrown away and had to
be read and decrypted all over again.

The measurements are in the commit, including the before-and-after frame rates
on identical scrolling.

## Verified

- Reproduced first, on an Android 15 emulator with a folder built for it, and
  re-measured after the fix with the same corpus and the same scrolling.
- Thumbnails written by 0.8.0 and earlier were confirmed to still open, with a
  test that seals a thumbnail the old way and reads it back through the new
  worker. That mattered: had it drifted, every cached thumbnail would have
  silently regenerated and nobody would have seen an error.
- Full suite green: **1,118 tests**, plus `flutter analyze` and `dart format`.

## Notes

- Windows builds are signed (Azure Artifact Signing); macOS builds are Developer
  ID signed and notarized; Android is release-signed.
- One occasional frame outlier remains under hard scrolling. An emulator is a
  poor instrument for single-frame timing, so it is being left for measurement on
  real hardware rather than guessed at.
