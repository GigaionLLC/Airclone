# Airclone v0.6.2 — store-correct updates, and three Android fixes

Updates now come from wherever you installed Airclone from, "Open in another app"
works on your own phone's files again, and importing an `rclone.conf` on Android
no longer strands you on a button you cannot reach. Airclone can also, if you ask
it to, survive being uninstalled.

## Updates come from the store you installed from

**Check for updates** now works out how this copy of Airclone was installed and
points at the right place:

- Installed from the **Microsoft Store**, **Google Play**, the **App Store**,
  **Flathub** or **Snap**? Airclone tells you updates come from there and offers
  to open it — and makes **no version request over the network at all**.
- Downloaded the installer, the portable zip, the `.dmg`, the tarball or an APK
  from the releases page? Nothing changes: Airclone checks GitHub, as before.

This was a Microsoft Store certification failure (policy 10.2.5): a Store build
must update only through the Store, and Airclone's update check offered an "Open
release" button that led to GitHub. Google Play and the App Store have the same
rule. The result is now a two-way choice the code cannot fall through — a store
build has no route to an out-of-store download.

## Android — "Open in another app" works on your own files

Opening a photo, video, or document that lives **on the phone itself** failed
with `Failed to find configured root that contains /storage/emulated/0/DCIM/…`.
Files from a cloud remote were always fine; local ones were not, because Airclone
hands them straight to the other app rather than making a pointless copy of a
40 GB video first — and that direct path had never been permitted.

Shared storage is now permitted, so local files open. A file on an SD card or a
USB stick is still outside what can be shared directly, so Airclone stages a copy
and hands that over instead — it works, at the cost of one local copy.

## Android — importing a config no longer looks broken

Importing an `rclone.conf` on a phone appeared to fail. The import itself always
worked: the **Merge** button was being drawn past the right edge of the screen,
so there was nothing to tap. Airclone's dialogs were sized for a desktop window
and were simply wider than a phone.

Every dialog now fits the screen it is on, and dialog buttons wrap onto a second
line instead of falling off the edge. Picking a file that cannot be read (from a
cloud provider, say) now tells you so, instead of spinning forever.

## Survive uninstall — optional, encrypted, off by default

Uninstalling Airclone on a phone destroys every remote you have configured. That
is deliberate: your credentials live in private app storage precisely so no
backup, no cable, and no other app can reach them. It is also a horrible
surprise, so there is now a way out of it.

**Settings → Config → Survive uninstall** keeps a copy of your remotes in a
folder outside the app, which uninstalling does not delete. Turning it on asks
you for a passphrase and encrypts the backup with it. Reinstall Airclone — or set
up a new phone — and it offers to bring your remotes back; you type the
passphrase, review exactly what is being restored, and confirm.

The backup keeps itself up to date whenever your remotes change. Turning the
setting off deletes the file.

You can also turn it on **without** a passphrase, because some people want a
plain `rclone.conf` they can copy to a computer. Airclone will tell you plainly
what that means — any app with file access can read it, device backups and
phone-to-phone transfers copy it — and make you confirm you understand. It is
never the default and never silent.

If you do not turn this on, nothing changes: Settings now simply says, above the
export buttons, that uninstalling loses your remotes.

## When something goes wrong: Settings → Diagnostics

Airclone sends no crash reports and no telemetry, which has always left a gap —
a bug report with no evidence is a bug that does not get fixed.

Problems are now recorded on your device, under **Settings → Diagnostics →
Problem report**, where you can read them and **copy** or **share** a report to
attach to a bug report. Passwords, tokens, keys, email addresses and your home
folder's name are stripped as each entry is recorded, so they are never in the
log in the first place. Nothing is written to a file unless you save or share
it, and nothing is ever sent anywhere.

## Notes

- Windows `airclone.exe` + installer are code-signed (Gigaion, LLC) and bundle a
  verified rclone **v1.75.0** engine; macOS is Developer ID signed + notarized;
  Android APKs are release-signed.
- "Survive uninstall" is **Android-only**. Desktop configs already live outside
  the app and survive reinstalling; iOS has no equivalent shared location, and
  Airclone has not shipped there yet.
- **Known limitation, unchanged from v0.6.0:** video *playback* is verified on
  desktop but could not be verified on Android hardware — the emulator cannot
  render video and no physical device was available. The preview *failure* paths
  are verified on Android; the success path is not.
